# Joel Dietz

**Applied AI Researcher · Computational Artist · Systems Architect · Founder**

I design systems for understanding and building **intelligence across scales**—from artificial agents and biological cells to markets, institutions, and cultures.

My work spans **artificial intelligence**, **machine consciousness**, **bioelectricity**, **collective intelligence**, **computational governance**, and **computational art**. Across these domains, I build research infrastructure, software, simulations, and artworks that explore how intelligent systems emerge, coordinate, adapt, and create.

Rather than separating science, engineering, and art, I see them as complementary approaches to the same questions. Research develops theory, software turns ideas into working systems, and art makes complex phenomena visible and experiential.

> **How do intelligent systems emerge, coordinate, adapt, and create—from cells and organisms to AI agents and human civilizations?**

# Research Areas

## 🧠 Artificial Intelligence & Machine Consciousness

Developing computational models of agency, active inference, self-modeling systems, cognitive architectures, evaluation frameworks, and machine consciousness.

## ⚡ Bioelectricity & Morphogenesis

Studying bioelectric signaling, cellular intelligence, regenerative biology, morphogenesis, programmable anatomy, and the computational principles underlying biological form.

## 🌐 Collective Intelligence & Computational Governance

Designing mechanisms for coordination, decentralized institutions, governance protocols, public goods, institutional design, and collective decision-making.

## 🔬 Complex Systems

Building simulations of biological, economic, ecological, and social systems to better understand emergence, adaptation, and large-scale coordination.

## 🎨 Computational Art

Creating generative artworks, audiovisual systems, symbolic interfaces, installations, and experimental media that explore complexity, emergence, mythology, consciousness, and transformation.

# Artistic Practice

My artistic practice explores many of the same themes as my research: emergence, intelligence, symbolism, transformation, and collective behavior.

Working across generative algorithms, photography, painting, AI, performance, and interactive media, I treat art as a research practice—a way of investigating the same questions that animate my work in artificial intelligence, bioelectricity, morphogenesis, and complex systems.

Mythology, alchemy, eros, speculative futures, and computation appear throughout my work not as historical references but as symbolic languages for thinking about transformation.

I don't see art and research as separate disciplines. Both are methods for discovering structures that already exist but have not yet become visible.
